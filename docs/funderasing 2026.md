## Assessment of the campaign ideas you proposed

The biggest conceptual problem is that the original plan treats everything as a list of content or fundraising ideas. A better system assigns each idea a job.

Some content should create **attention**. Some should create **trust**. Some should generate **money directly**. Some should create **corporate leads**. Some should generate **FTC outreach evidence**. Those are different functions.

### Storytelling and social content

| Idea | Assessment | Recommended role |
|---|---|---|
| **“Đằng sau tấm huy chương”** | **Keep and prioritize.** This is one of the strongest ideas. | Trust + emotional proof. End every story with one concrete current-season need rather than merely celebrating history. |
| **“Chuyện linh kiện”** | **Make this a core fundraising series.** | Direct conversion. Turn components into understandable donation units. |
| **“Học sinh cấp 3 làm Robotics có gì?”** | **Keep.** | Humanize the team, recruitment, shareability, parent/alumni engagement. |
| **Guess-the-component / code mini-games** | Useful, but secondary. | Reach and interaction—not a fundraising pillar. |
| **POV / trend TikToks and Reels** | Use selectively. | Top-of-funnel awareness. Avoid becoming a meme account disconnected from the funding story. |
| **Q&A / livestream** | Weak as a recurring format unless audience already exists. | Use around meaningful moments: robot reveal, workshop, milestone, competition announcement. |
| **“Robot succeeds → donate” challenge** | Very good. | Eventized donation spike; make the goal and donation amount visible on-screen. |

The biggest improvement is **CTA discipline**.

A post should not end with:

> “Hãy đồng hành cùng Robotics Sóc Sơn!”

That is emotionally positive but operationally weak.

It should end with something like:

> **“Mục tiêu tuần này: 3.500.000đ cho vật tư drivetrain. QR ở slide cuối. 100% khoản ủng hộ tuần này được cập nhật trong báo cáo quỹ.”**

or:

> **“Bạn làm việc tại một công ty công nghệ/sản xuất có chương trình CSR? Gửi bài này cho phòng HR/Marketing hoặc giới thiệu chúng mình qua [contact].”**

The second CTA is extremely important. FIRST’s own grant guidance recommends approaching local businesses and specifically suggests that mentors and parents ask their employers whether they can support the team. citeturn7search1 Your **network can be more valuable than your follower count**.

### Merchandise

The STEM-merch ideas are good, but this should **not** become a mini retail business that consumes the Media team.

My ranking would be:

**Sticker pack → bookmark → one wearable item → component keychain.**

The sticker pack is ideal because it reinforces identity and can contain inside jokes such as:

> “Code không lỗi đời không nể”  
> “Fix bug xuyên đêm”  
> “Works on my robot”  
> “Ctrl + Z không cứu được hardware”  
> “Không phải lỗi code, tại field”

The QR bookmark is also smart because the merchandise becomes a distribution channel for your best robot video.

For apparel, use **pre-orders only** in the first wave. Do not spend fundraising capital manufacturing speculative inventory.

For the “robot-part keychain,” I would slightly change the idea: make a designed **“Robot Fragment” collectible** from laser-cut acrylic, 3D-printed geometry, or truly unusable scrap. Do not consume reusable FTC hardware for merch.

Most importantly, merchandise should carry a clear funding message:

> **“Mỗi sản phẩm = một phần chi phí chế tạo robot BIOBUZZ 2026–2027.”**

Not just “CLB merch.”

The acoustic night / mini-fair is much lower priority. It can work **only if another club handles most of the event production**. Robotics Sóc Sơn should contribute a robot experience booth, merch, and fundraising CTA rather than becoming an event-management organization.

### Sponsorship

This is where the campaign can improve most.

Your old proposal has the correct ingredients—logo placement, media exposure, workshops, transparency, reporting—but most benefits are still described generically. fileciteturn0file3

Strong current FTC-team sponsorship models increasingly make the exchange concrete. WLMAC Robotics publishes a full season budget, clear sponsorship tiers, accepts in-kind contributions at equivalent value, and offers higher-tier partners a robot demo and sponsor showcase. citeturn9search9 Wilsonic Boom similarly shows sponsors exactly what specific equipment costs, includes update emails and demonstrations, accepts supplier credit, and confirms sponsorship terms in writing before payment. citeturn9search8 Mainframe Robotics and other FTC teams differentiate higher tiers through robot placement, sponsor spotlight content, media recognition, and company demonstrations. citeturn9search0turn9search2

A Vietnamese precedent exists too: ThunderClaw publicly recognizes both financial and material sponsors, including BIDV and in-kind support. citeturn9search14

So I would replace the old Bronze/Silver/Gold/Diamond structure with **three easy-to-understand corporate levels plus custom in-kind support**:

| Suggested level | Contribution | What they receive |
|---|---:|---|
| **Community Partner** | ~3M VND | Sponsor webpage, season thank-you post, logo/name in season recap, digital certificate/thank-you package |
| **Build Partner** | ~10M VND | Everything above + logo on team/event materials, dedicated sponsor story, mid-season update, sponsor included in workshop/demo materials |
| **Season Partner** | ~25M VND+ | Everything above + prominent robot/pit/apparel placement where permitted, live robot demonstration at sponsor location, end-of-season impact report, invitation to team showcase |
| **Technical / In-kind Partner** | Custom | Parts, CNC/laser cutting, 3D printing, tools, workspace, transport, printing, media production, food, shipping, professional mentoring |

Those numbers are **recommended starting anchors**, not externally mandated market rates. Adjust them after you calculate the actual 2026–2027 funding gap.

Do not promise benefits that depend on competition organizers or school authorization. Robot branding, co-branding, payment documentation, and any public use of sponsor logos should be confirmed with the school and checked against current competition requirements before signing the agreement.

## The campaign I would run instead

I would call the entire season fundraising campaign something stronger than “Fundraising 2026.”

### **BUILD THE NEXT ROBOT**
### *Mỗi mảnh ghép, một bước tiến.*

This connects directly to your excellent “Chuyện linh kiện” concept.

The entire campaign then operates through **three fundraising engines**.

### Corporate partnerships

This should generate the largest individual contributions.

Your sponsor pitch should be only one page initially—not the full sponsorship dossier.

The first-page narrative:

> **Robotics Sóc Sơn is building for FTC BIOBUZZ 2026–2027.**
>
> We are students from Sóc Sơn High School who have competed nationally in robotics and are now taking on a new international-standard engineering challenge.
>
> We are seeking companies that can contribute funding, manufacturing support, components, tools, logistics, or professional mentoring.
>
> **Here is exactly what your support builds.**

Then show:

**Need → cost → sponsor → result.**

Examples:

> **Motor & motion system → X VND → reliable drivetrain → more testing hours.**

> **3D printing / CNC sponsor → in-kind → faster prototyping → more iterations before competition.**

> **Workshop sponsor → X VND → Y students experience robotics → documented STEM community impact.**

That is vastly stronger than “Gold sponsor gets a large logo.”

Your target list should not begin with random cold emails. Build outward from:

**parents → alumni → teachers → parents’ employers → alumni employers → local SMEs → industrial/manufacturing firms → banks → technology firms → component suppliers → education businesses.**

The school’s long alumni history makes this particularly attractive as a warm-introduction strategy. citeturn2search13

### Community fundraising

This is where storytelling, robot challenges, “Chuyện linh kiện,” and progress updates work.

Do **not** simply show a single huge total such as:

> “We need 70,000,000 VND.”

Break it into visible missions.

For example:

> **Mission: drivetrain**

> Goal: 8,000,000đ  
> Raised: 5,200,000đ  
> Remaining: 2,800,000đ

Then:

> **Mission: sensors**

> **Mission: prototypes**

> **Mission: competition transport**

Smaller goals make it easier for a donor to understand what their contribution is accomplishing.

Use a public funding tracker with three statuses:

**Funded / In progress / Still needed.**

The best “Chuyện linh kiện” posts then become fundraising pages in miniature:

> **“This tiny encoder tells a robot exactly how far it has moved. Without accurate feedback, autonomous code is partly guessing.”**

Then show:

> Cost: ___  
> Need: ___  
> Funded: ___  
> **Sponsor this component → QR**

This is also a much better implementation of the message you already proposed:

> “Một cỗ máy mạnh mẽ được dựng nên từ hàng trăm mảnh ghép nhỏ — sự đóng góp của bạn chính là mảnh ghép quan trọng tiếp theo.”

### Earned fundraising

Here, supporters receive a real product or experience:

**Maker Day + pre-order merch.**

This reduces dependence on pure donations and simultaneously generates outreach.

The workshop should be repeated rather than run once. For example:

**Maker Day A → beginner robot driver**

**Maker Day B → circuits & sensors**

**Maker Day C → intro programming**

You do not need three at launch. Build one repeatable module first.

Merch should run in limited “drops,” not continuous inventory.

A strong model is:

> **Drop: BUILD THE NEXT ROBOT / Season 01**

Sticker set + bookmark + optional shirt/tote.

Each order page tells the buyer what the profit supports.

I would **not manufacture a fake urgency date** yet. The weekly media workload can stay surprisingly small:

**One emotional/story post + one technical/progress post + one short-form video + Stories showing daily build life.**

Every piece does not need to ask for money. The sequence should look like:

> **Proof → story → need → ask → progress → gratitude → proof.**

For example:

**Post A — Story**

> *“Chiếc robot dừng hoạt động 40 phút trước trận đấu.”*

Tell the actual story, then:

> “Mùa BIOBUZZ này, chúng mình đang xây một hệ thống đáng tin cậy hơn.”

**Post B — Component**

> *“Thứ nhỏ nhất trên robot lại quyết định robot biết mình đang ở đâu.”*

Show encoder/sensor → explain → cost → QR.

**Post C — Human**

> *“Học sinh cấp 3 làm robotics đánh đổi điều gì?”*

Three 10-second answers.

**Post D — Progress**

> “72% mục tiêu drivetrain đã được tài trợ.”

Show names/logos of consenting supporters.

**Post E — Sponsor**

> “10 triệu đồng thực sự làm được gì với một đội robotics?”

Show the actual funded subsystem or workshop impact.

That last format is especially valuable for corporate conversion because it demonstrates that sponsorship produces visible outcomes.

> **BIOBUZZ creates the urgency.**

> **Past achievements create credibility.**

> **Students and engineering stories create emotional connection.**

> **The delta budget creates trust.**
