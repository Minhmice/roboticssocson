## Drivetrain Architectures and Kinematics in FIRST Tech Challenge

The drivetrain chassis serves as the physical and mechanical foundation of an FTC robot, determining translational agility, pushing force, and trajectory precision during both 30-second autonomous and 2-minute teleoperated periods. Selecting the optimal drive architecture—Mecanum, Tank, or Coaxial Swerve—and mastering inverse kinematics and dead-wheel odometry algorithms is essential for elite match play.

### Comparative Analysis of FTC Drivetrain Configurations

Drivetrain configurations present distinct engineering trade-offs regarding mechanical complexity, friction characteristics, and software control overhead:

| Drivetrain Type | Holonomic Mobility | Drive Actuator Count | Traction & Pushing Force | Mechanical Complexity | Software & Tuning Difficulty | Primary Match Application |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mecanum Drive | Yes (Omnidirectional translation & rotation) | 4 independent planetary motors | Moderate (Susceptible to lateral defensive pushing) | Low (Direct axle mounting to gearboxes) | Moderate (Standard Mecanum kinematic model) | Standard FTC baseline; excels at agile cycling and scoring alignment |
| Tank / Skid-Steer | No (Non-holonomic; forward, reverse, tank turn) | 2, 4, or 6 gearmotors | Very High (High-durometer roughtop or rubber tread) | Minimal (Spur gears or timing belt pulleys) | Low (Direct differential drive control) | Defense-heavy matches, obstacle traversal, heavy chassis designs |
| Coaxial Swerve | Yes (Independent steering angle & drive velocity per pod) | 4 drive + 4 steer (Brushless or Smart Servos) | High (Treaded wheels with active steering vectoring) | Extreme (Bevel gear differentials, slip rings/continuous wiring) | High (Individual pod azimuth PID + vector field odometry) | Cutting-edge high-resource teams seeking uncompromised traction and maneuverability |
| 3 / 4-Wheel Omni | Yes (90° perpendicular passive rollers) | 3 or 4 motors | Low (Low lateral resistance) | Low | Moderate | Ultra-lightweight chassis requiring rapid circular agility |

*Crucial Mecanum Roller Geometry:* Mecanum wheels must be installed in an **"X-Pattern" configuration when viewed from top-down**. The contact rollers touching the mat floor must angle inward toward the geometric center of the robot. If accidentally mounted in an "O-Pattern", the lateral force vectors cancel destructively, eliminating strafing capability.

### Mecanum Inverse Kinematics Formulation

To command a robot with longitudinal velocity `v_x`, lateral velocity `v_y`, and angular yaw rate `omega_z` (rad/s) in the robot coordinate frame, target wheel surface linear velocities are computed through the classic kinematic matrix:


```text
v_{FL} = v_x - v_y - omega_z (L_x + L_y)
```



```text
v_{FR} = v_x + v_y + omega_z (L_x + L_y)
```



```text
v_{BL} = v_x + v_y - omega_z (L_x + L_y)
```



```text
v_{BR} = v_x - v_y + omega_z (L_x + L_y)
```


Where:
- `L_x`: Longitudinal half-track distance from chassis centroid to wheel axle centers (m).
- `L_y`: Lateral half-track distance from chassis centroid to wheel contact centerlines (m).
- `v_FL, v_FR, v_BL, v_BR`: Commanded linear velocity targets for Front-Left, Front-Right, Back-Left, and Back-Right wheels.

#### Power Saturation Normalization
During compound maneuvers (strafing while rotating under full forward throttle), individual wheel commands can exceed the hardware ceiling `[-1.0, 1.0]`. Normalizing against the maximum component preserves heading vectors and avoids unpredictable trajectory distortion:


```text
v_{max} = max(|v_{FL}|, |v_{FR}|, |v_{BL}|, |v_{BR}|, 1.0)
```



```text
v_i' = (v_i / v_{max)} quad forall i in {FL, FR, BL, BR}
```


### Field-Centric Coordinate Frame Transformation

Field-centric drive control decouples joystick directions from chassis rotation, allowing operators to drive relative to the field perimeter regardless of where the robot intake points. Raw gamepad joystick inputs (`J_x, J_y`) are rotated into the robot coordinate frame using current IMU yaw angle `theta`:


```text
v_x = J_x cos(-theta) - J_y sin(-theta)
```



```text
v_y = J_x sin(-theta) + J_y cos(-theta)
```


### Dead-Wheel Odometry Kinematics

Because Mecanum passive rollers experience intermittent slippage during aggressive accelerations, drive motor encoders cannot provide deterministic pose tracking for autonomous routines. FTC teams implement **3-wheel unpowered dead-wheel odometry pods** pressed into the soft foam tiles via sprung suspensions.

```text
       ┌─────────── Front Chassis Frame ───────────┐
       │                                           │
  Left Dead Wheel (L)                     Right Dead Wheel (R)
       │                                           │
       │         Perpendicular Dead Wheel (C)      │
       └───────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Pose updates across discrete sample intervals `Delta t` follow arc-length approximations:


```text
Delta theta = (Delta L - Delta R / B)
```



```text
Delta y_{local} = (Delta L + Delta R / 2)
```



```text
Delta x_{local} = Delta C - D_{perp} * Delta theta
```


Where:
- `B`: Track width between left and right tracking wheels (calibrated in mm).
- `D_perp`: Perpendicular displacement from robot center of rotation to the transverse tracking wheel.
- `Delta L, Delta R, Delta C`: Incremental linear arc displacements reported by high-resolution encoders (such as REV Through-Bore Encoders).

### Complete Java Implementation: Field-Centric Mecanum Controller

```java
package org.firstinspires.ftc.teamcode;

import com.qualcomm.hardware.rev.RevHubOrientationOnRobot;
import com.qualcomm.robotcore.eventloop.opmode.LinearOpMode;
import com.qualcomm.robotcore.eventloop.opmode.TeleOp;
import com.qualcomm.robotcore.hardware.DcMotor;
import com.qualcomm.robotcore.hardware.DcMotorEx;
import com.qualcomm.robotcore.hardware.IMU;
import org.firstinspires.ftc.robotcore.external.navigation.AngleUnit;

@TeleOp(name = "Field-Centric Mecanum Controller", group = "Guides")
public class FieldCentricMecanumController extends LinearOpMode {
    @Override
    public void runOpMode() {
        DcMotorEx motorFL = hardwareMap.get(DcMotorEx.class, "motorFL");
        DcMotorEx motorFR = hardwareMap.get(DcMotorEx.class, "motorFR");
        DcMotorEx motorBL = hardwareMap.get(DcMotorEx.class, "motorBL");
        DcMotorEx motorBR = hardwareMap.get(DcMotorEx.class, "motorBR");

        // Reverse left motors to align positive power with forward chassis motion
        motorFL.setDirection(DcMotor.Direction.REVERSE);
        motorBL.setDirection(DcMotor.Direction.REVERSE);
        motorFR.setDirection(DcMotor.Direction.FORWARD);
        motorBR.setDirection(DcMotor.Direction.FORWARD);

        // Active electromagnetic braking for immediate stopping
        motorFL.setZeroPowerBehavior(DcMotor.ZeroPowerBehavior.BRAKE);
        motorFR.setZeroPowerBehavior(DcMotor.ZeroPowerBehavior.BRAKE);
        motorBL.setZeroPowerBehavior(DcMotor.ZeroPowerBehavior.BRAKE);
        motorBR.setZeroPowerBehavior(DcMotor.ZeroPowerBehavior.BRAKE);

        IMU imu = hardwareMap.get(IMU.class, "imu");
        IMU.Parameters imuConfig = new IMU.Parameters(new RevHubOrientationOnRobot(
                RevHubOrientationOnRobot.LogoFacingDirection.UP,
                RevHubOrientationOnRobot.UsbFacingDirection.FORWARD));
        imu.initialize(imuConfig);

        waitForStart();

        while (opModeIsActive()) {
            double y = -gamepad1.left_stick_y; // Invert joystick Y convention
            double x = gamepad1.left_stick_x * 1.1; // Counteract lateral roller drag
            double rx = gamepad1.right_stick_x;

            if (gamepad1.guide || gamepad1.options) {
                imu.resetYaw();
            }

            double headingRadians = imu.getRobotYawPitchRollAngles().getYaw(AngleUnit.RADIANS);

            // Vector rotation into robot-centric frame
            double rotX = x * Math.cos(-headingRadians) - y * Math.sin(-headingRadians);
            double rotY = x * Math.sin(-headingRadians) + y * Math.cos(-headingRadians);

            // Mecanum inverse kinematics calculation
            double maxComponent = Math.max(Math.abs(rotY) + Math.abs(rotX) + Math.abs(rx), 1.0);
            double flPower = (rotY + rotX + rx) / maxComponent;
            double blPower = (rotY - rotX + rx) / maxComponent;
            double frPower = (rotY - rotX - rx) / maxComponent;
            double brPower = (rotY + rotX - rx) / maxComponent;

            motorFL.setPower(flPower);
            motorBL.setPower(blPower);
            motorFR.setPower(frPower);
            motorBR.setPower(brPower);

            telemetry.addData("Heading Angle (Deg)", Math.toDegrees(headingRadians));
            telemetry.addData("FL / FR Power", "%.2f / %.2f", flPower, frPower);
            telemetry.addData("BL / BR Power", "%.2f / %.2f", blPower, brPower);
            telemetry.update();
        }
    }
}
```

### Diagnostic Field Procedures and Maintenance

- **Unwanted Turning Drift While Driving Straight:** Calculate the physical Center of Mass (CoM). If the battery pack or heavy lift arm biases weight heavily toward one side, the high-normal-force wheels exhibit greater tractive resistance, causing yaw deflection. Counterbalance the chassis or implement closed-loop gyro heading lock in software.
- **Odometry Tracking Wheel Bounce:** Foam tiles compress under robot weight. Ensure tracking pods use constant-force springs calibrated between 2.0 N and 3.0 N. Excessive spring force lifts the chassis off the floor, whereas insufficient preload causes the wheel to lose contact during rapid direction reversals.
- **Drivetrain Sluggishness Under Low Battery:** Monitor internal battery sensor logs. If battery voltage drops under 10.5V, high current draw during 4-motor acceleration pushes the hub toward brownout cutoffs. Replace battery packs every 2–3 match cycles.
