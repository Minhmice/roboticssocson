## Overview of the FTC Judging Interview

The judging interview in the FIRST Tech Challenge represents the primary opportunity for teams to articulate their engineering design process, software innovations, collaborative teamwork, and STEM community outreach. Awards like the Inspire Award, Think Award, Innovate Award, Design Award, Control Award, Connect Award, and Motivate Award rely heavily on the interview alongside the team's Engineering Portfolio.

A typical judging session runs for 10 to 15 minutes in a private room with a panel of volunteer judges. The standard agenda consists of:
1. **5-Minute Uninterrupted Presentation:** The team delivers their rehearsed pitch covering all facets of their season.
2. **5 to 10 Minutes Technical Q&A:** Judges ask detailed technical and process questions regarding robot design, failure modes, coding architectures, and outreach metrics.

## 5-Minute Pitch Structure and Time Allocation

Novice teams often spend excessive time detailing team history or focusing exclusively on a single sub-mechanism. A structured breakdown ensures balanced coverage across mechanical engineering, software control, and outreach initiatives:

| Segment | Duration | Primary Focus | Visual Artifact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Hook & Introduction** | 30 seconds | Team identity, season thesis, headline robot metric | Team logo banner, robot branding |
| **2. Mechanical Design & CAD** | 90 seconds | Chassis layout, intake/outtake mechanics, iteration cycle | Exploded CAD renders, broken prototype iterations |
| **3. Autonomous & Control Systems** | 75 seconds | Odometry localization, motion profiling, computer vision | Telemetry plots, vision target overlay captures |
| **4. Outreach & Sustainability** | 60 seconds | Community workshops, team budget, sponsorship model | Engineering Portfolio outreach page, sponsor map |
| **5. Conclusion & Q&A Handoff** | 45 seconds | Next milestones, gratitude, direct transition to Q&A | Open table of contents in Portfolio, eye contact |

## Technical Q&A Handling: The STAR Matrix

Judges evaluate whether students independently developed solutions or relied passively on mentors. Teams should format responses using an engineering-driven three-tier framework:

1. **Problem and Constraints:** Clarify the physical or computational bottleneck encountered (e.g., intake slippage during rapid directional transitions).
2. **Quantitative Trade-off:** Explain why specific design alternatives were rejected in favor of the current iteration based on data (e.g., motor stall torque, durometer ratings, amp draw).
3. **Validation and Iteration:** Present the measurable result recorded in the Engineering Portfolio after deploying the modification.

## Visual Aids and Team Non-Verbal Dynamics

- **Central Robot Placement:** Keep the robot accessible on the center table. Ensure power is safely disengaged while mechanisms remain free to articulate by hand during explanations.
- **Physical Prototypes on Hand:** Bring failed 3D printed brackets, stripped gears, or bent carbon fiber links. Passing physical iterations to judges demonstrates continuous improvement.
- **Shared Speaking Roles:** Do not let a single spokesperson dominate. Ensure mechanical, programming, driving, and business leads speak directly to their respective specialties.
- **Handling Unknowns:** When asked a question outside the scope of current testing, reply honestly: *"We have not benchmarked that configuration yet due to time constraints, but our working hypothesis is... and it is queued for our post-qualifier sprint."*
