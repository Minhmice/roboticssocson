## FTC Alliance Selection Protocol and Rules

Following the conclusion of the qualification matches, the top 4 ranked teams become Alliance Captains (Captains 1 through 4). Alliance Selection precedes the elimination bracket and directly determines tournament championship trajectories.

Official FTC Alliance Selection adheres to strict tournament governance:
1. **Calling Order:** Alliance Captain 1 calls first, followed in sequential order by Captains 2, 3, and 4.
2. **The Decline Rule:** If Team B is invited by Alliance Captain A and declines the invitation, Team B is barred from being invited by any other alliance for the remainder of the draft. Team B may only continue in the tournament if they are ranked high enough to serve as an Alliance Captain themselves.
3. **The Slide-Up Dynamic:** If a top-4 ranked team accepts an invitation from a higher-seeded alliance, the 5th-ranked team elevates to assume the role of Alliance Captain 4.

## Constructing an Empirical Picklist

Competitive teams never take the selection stage without an empirically ordered picklist ranked 1 through N based on scouting data. Selection criteria must focus on **mechanical complementarity** rather than nominal leaderboard rankings:

| Strategic Vector | Weight | Evaluation Parameter | Elimination Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Autonomous Compatibility** | 30% | Coordinate collision risk and route flexibility | Simultaneous auto routines scoring maximum pre-loads without interference |
| **TeleOp Cycle Velocity** | 35% | Normalized cycles completed per match | High sustained element throughput while withstanding opponent defensive pinning |
| **Endgame Rigging Reliability** | 20% | Suspension cycle duration (sub-15 seconds) | Guaranteed endgame points executed cleanly before match clock expiration |
| **Defensive Resistance** | 15% | Robot mass, traction wheel setup, low center of mass | Ability to hold field position against aggressive opposing defensive screening |

## Contingency Trees and Real-Time Decision Matrix

When holding the Captain 1 seed, the conventional opening move is extending an invitation to Captain 2 to establish a dominant alliance. However, if Captain 2 declines to lead their own bracket or Captain 1 prefers a complementary specialist, an immediate contingency sequence activates:

```
                      [Alliance Captain 1]
                               │
               Invite Captain 2? ─┼─ No (Select Highest Picklist Specialist)
                               │
         ┌─────────────────────┴─────────────────────┐
         ▼                                           ▼
     [Accepted]                                  [Declined]
 ┌─────────────────┐                     ┌─────────────────────────┐
 │ Dominant Seed   │                     │ Captain 2 Locked Out    │
 │ Bracket Set     │                     │ Call Picklist Rank 1    │
 └─────────────────┘                     └─────────────────────────┘
```

Prior to the alliance selection ceremony, the Strategy Lead must conduct pit audits with prospective top-tier partners to verify three critical variables:
- **Battery Health and Charge Status:** Ensure the candidate possesses sufficient fresh packs exceeding 13.5V for rapid playoff turnaround.
- **Drive Team Temperament:** Confirm that the partner's drive team communicates cleanly and takes directional coaching during high-stress matches.
- **Autonomous Script Flexibility:** Verify that the partner has calibrated alternative autonomous variants (left/right starting positions, timed delays).

## Fatal Strategic Errors to Avoid

1. **Social Picking:** Selecting familiar friend teams whose robots exhibit chronic Wi-Fi Direct disconnects or fragile mechanism linkages.
2. **Scoring Zone Chokepoints:** Partnering with a robot that demands the exact same sub-field scoring space, causing gridlock during TeleOp cycling.
3. **Neglecting Opponent Counter-Play:** Entering playoffs with lightweight, high-center-of-gravity builds that tip over under heavy field defense.
4. **Panic Calling on Decline:** Freezing when a primary pick unexpectedly declines, resulting in an unvetted selection made under a ticking clock.
