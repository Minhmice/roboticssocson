## The Role of Quantitative Scouting in FTC

Scouting in the FIRST Tech Challenge is not merely casual observation from the stands; it is the structured collection, normalization, and statistical analysis of field data. A robust scouting operation serves two tactical objectives:
1. **Qualification Match Pre-planning:** Identifying synergy, cycle paths, and spatial bottlenecks between two alliance partners before stepping onto the playing field.
2. **Playoff Alliance Selection Picklist Architecture:** Constructing an empirical picklist based on verified scoring rates, eliminating recency bias and subjective guesswork.

## Quantitative Scouting Metrics and Data Collection Form

A standardized scouting sheet separates gameplay into distinct game periods and isolates key mechanical and operational attributes:

| Category | Primary Metric | Calculation / Tracking Method | Strategic Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Autonomous** | Pre-load & Spike Success Rate | `(Successful Scored Pre-loads / Total Match Attempts) * 100%` | Validates computer vision reliability and dead-reckoning odometry accuracy |
| **Autonomous** | Parking / Navigation Consistency | Discrete point value achieved per match | Confirms ability to avoid colliding with alliance partners during transit |
| **TeleOp** | Scoring Cycle Time | `Total TeleOp Active Driving Time (seconds) / Valid Elements Scored` | Reflects intake reliability, driver dexterity, and outtake velocity |
| **TeleOp** | Element Drop & Miss Frequency | `Dropped Elements / (Acquired Elements + Dropped Elements)` | Highlights grip stability under violent lateral acceleration |
| **Endgame** | Rigging Hang / Suspension Speed | Suspension level achieved and seconds elapsed to lock mechanism | Establishes critical point deltas in the final 30 seconds of the match |
| **Discipline** | Penalty Propensity | Minor and Major penalty points conceded per match | Filters out high-scoring teams whose foul points negate net alliance margin |

## Data Analysis & Component Offensive Power Rating (cOPR)

To isolate individual team contributions within a two-team alliance, teams construct a system of linear equations modeling Offensive Power Rating (OPR):

```
[Match 1]: Team_A + Team_B = Alliance_1_Score
[Match 2]: Team_A + Team_C = Alliance_2_Score
...
[Match N]: Team_X + Team_Y = Alliance_N_Score
```

Because total OPR can be skewed by opponent penalty points or partner disconnects, advanced scouts compute **Component OPR (cOPR)** by breaking match totals into separate vectors for Autonomous scoring, TeleOp cycle output, and Endgame suspension points.

Operational consistency is evaluated using the standard deviation (`\sigma`) of TeleOp cycle counts:
- `\sigma < 1.5`: High reliability, predictable cycle output, low mechanical failure risk.
- `\sigma > 4.0`: High variance, frequently prone to jammed intakes, loose belt tension, or software disconnects.

## Pre-Match Alliance Coordination and Game Strategy

Fifteen minutes prior to match queuing, the Drive Coach must meet with the alliance partner to establish concrete operational boundaries:

1. **Autonomous Trajectory De-confliction:**
   - Map tile-by-tile coordinate vectors for both robots during the 30-second autonomous window.
   - If paths overlap in the central depot or scoring backdrop, the team with the lower autonomous cOPR executes a secondary failsafe script (safe park only).

2. **TeleOp Traffic Lane Allocation:**
   - Designate one robot to the exterior perimeter corridor and the other to the interior lane.
   - Right-of-Way Rule: The loaded robot returning to the scoring target maintains absolute right-of-way over the empty robot traveling to retrieve game elements.

3. **Emergency Contingency Protocols:**
   - In the event of partner disconnection or mechanical stall in the field corridor, the active robot immediately shifts to solo cycling without attempting hazardous pushes that could cause entanglement penalties.
