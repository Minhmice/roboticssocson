## 6-Month FTC Season Timeline and Milestone Planning

![Engineering project planning board and robotics subsystem prototyping](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1531403009284-440f080d1e12?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

A standard FIRST Tech Challenge campaign spans approximately six months, initiating with the global game release (Kickoff in early September) and culminating in regional Qualifiers and National Championships between January and March. Teams rarely fail because of a lack of innovative ideas; they stumble due to poor schedule pacing. Completing a robot days before a tournament leaves drivers unpracticed and autonomous algorithms fragile. Structured sprint planning with technical freeze dates is essential for championship contention.

### Battle-Tested 6-Month Roadmap: Kickoff to Championship

The following six-month progression provides predictable operational checkpoints across all sub-teams:

| Phase | Calendar Window | Strategic Focus Area | Tangible Milestone Deliverables |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Month 1** | September | Rules analysis, strategic scoring math, cardboard/wood prototypes | Scoring priority matrix, completed drivetrain chassis, proof-of-concept mockups |
| **Month 2** | October | Robot V1 fabrication, electrical harness routing, sensor bring-up | Driving baseline robot, functional intake/lift, cleanly harnessed wire bundles |
| **Month 3** | November | Autonomous trajectory generation, cycle time benchmarks, scrimmage | `30\texts` autonomous routine with `\ge 70\%` success rate, scrimmage match logs |
| **Month 4** | December | Robot V2 iteration, weight reduction, intensive driver practice | Swapping 3D parts for CNC aluminum/polycarb, logging `\ge 20` driver practice hours |
| **Month 5** | January | Regional Qualifier 1 competition, post-match analysis, bug triage | Official match performance, finalized 15-page portfolio, failure mode corrections |
| **Month 6** | February–March | Championship optimization, autonomous repeatability, documentation | Autonomous routines reaching `\ge 90\%` repeatability, critical spare parts inventory |

### Strict Technical Freeze Milestones

Uncontrolled iterations immediately prior to competition induce catastrophic failures. Enforce three non-negotiable freeze barriers:

```
[Kickoff: Month 0]
       |
       v  (4 Weeks: Strategy & Chassis Bring-up)
[Milestone 1: Code Freeze V1 - End of October]
       |  -> Chassis geometry locked; robot handed to software for initial telemetry
       v  (4 Weeks: Autonomous Routines & Subsystem Refinements)
[Milestone 2: Mechanical Freeze V2 - Mid-December]
       |  -> Major CAD redesigns prohibited; only maintenance & wear-part swaps permitted
       v  (3-4 Weeks: Driver Practice & Stress Testing)
[Milestone 3: Final System Freeze - 10 Days Before Event]
          -> Zero hardware or software commits; focus strictly on drive drills & pit prep
```

### Risk Buffers and Spare Subsystem Inventory

- **Two-Week Buffer Mandate:** Any experimental intake or lift revision must be fully validated at least 14 days before a competition. If it misses this cutoff, immediately roll back to the previously verified baseline.
- **Critical Spares Production:** Every custom load-bearing 3D printed component, gear cluster, hex axle, and timing belt must have at least two identical drop-in replacements ready in the pit toolbox.
- **Drive Team Practice Target:** Clock a minimum of 20 verified hours on full tile fields before the first qualifier. Alliance victory is determined by driver spatial awareness, cycle consistency, and stress recovery.

### Weekly Sprint Operating Cadence

Maintaining a predictable weekly operational loop keeps the engineering cycle transparent:

1. **Monday (Sprint Planning):** 30-minute review of the Kanban backlog, assigning task owners across Mechanical, Electrical, Programming, and Documentation tracks.
2. **Wednesday–Thursday (Mid-Week Lab Work):** Bench fabrication, logging sensor telemetry and calibration curves directly into the Engineering Notebook.
3. **Saturday–Sunday (Full Integration & Retrospective):** Full-field scrimmage runs, cycle-time telemetry recording, video breakdown of mechanical failures, and end-of-week sprint retrospective.
