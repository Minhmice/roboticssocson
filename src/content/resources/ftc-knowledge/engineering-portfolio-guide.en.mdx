## Building a Winning 15-Page FTC Engineering Portfolio

In the FIRST Tech Challenge (FTC) judging system, the Engineering Portfolio is the single most decisive document for prestigious judged awards such as the Inspire Award, Think Award, Innovate Award, and Design Award. Because judges have mere minutes during their initial deliberation rounds to digest your season's work, structuring your 15 pages with dense quantitative evidence, clear visual hierarchies, and rigorous adherence to FIRST guidelines is paramount.

![Engineering blueprints, CAD designs, and technical notebooks](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1504384308090-c894fdcc538d?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

### 15-Page Strategic Layout and Rubric Alignment

FIRST enforces an uncompromising constraint: judges are instructed to discard or ignore any pages beyond the 15-page limit. To capture maximum scoring across multiple award rubrics, allocate your page budget deliberately:

```text
[Page 1]       Team Identity, Member Roles & Season Engineering Goals
[Pages 2-8]    Engineering Design Process (EDP) per Subsystem (Drivetrain, Intake, Slides, Claw)
[Pages 9-11]   Software Architecture, Autonomous Odometry & Motion Control (Control Award)
[Pages 12-13]  Community Outreach, Mentorship & STEM Industry Connections (Connect / Motivate)
[Pages 14-15]  Team Sustainability, Budget Management & Strategic Growth (Business Plan)
```

| Portfolio Section | Recommended Budget | Target Judging Rubric | Essential Evidence Required |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Team Identity & Summary** | 1 Page | Culture & Gracious Professionalism | Member roster, individual responsibilities, overarching season goals |
| **Mechanical EDP Iterations** | 6 – 7 Pages | Think, Innovate, Design Awards | CAD cross-sections, trade studies, load-testing graphs, failure logs |
| **Software & Control Systems** | 2 – 3 Pages | Control Award | State machine flowcharts, PIDF tuning step responses, vision pipelines |
| **Outreach & Professional Connect** | 2 Pages | Connect & Motivate Awards | Quantitative community reach, engineering mentor logs, student impact |
| **Business Plan & Sustainability** | 2 Pages | Strategic Longevity | Bill of materials (BOM), cash-flow balance sheet, sponsor pipeline |

### Documenting the Engineering Design Process (EDP)

Judges do not reward robots that appear magically flawless on day one; they reward documented proof of **learning through iterative failure**:
1. **The Four-Stage Subsystem Narrative**: For every primary robot mechanism, document four distinct evolutionary stages:
   - **Problem Formulation**: Specific mechanical requirements (e.g., target reach height, payload mass, cycle time threshold).
   - **Preliminary Prototyping (V1)**: Initial laser-cut plywood or 3D-printed proofs of concept.
   - **Root-Cause Failure Analysis**: Honest dissection of design shortcomings (e.g., cable binding under off-axis load, gear stripping).
   - **Engineered Refinement (V2/V3)**: Final machined aluminum geometry, bearing support additions, and measured empirical benchmark improvements.
2. **Quantify Everything**: Replace subjective statements like *"Our intake improved significantly"* with verified engineering metrics: *"Intake acquisition latency decreased from 2.4s (V1 standard servo) to 0.42s (V3 compliant Shore 95A TPU wheels paired with a laser ToF distance trigger)"*.

### Visual Formatting and Pre-Submission Quality Control

- **Information Density via Visual Callouts**: Replace dense text walls with exploded CAD renderings featuring numbered callout boxes pointing directly to key engineering innovations.
- **Typographic Discipline**: Maintain a minimum legible font size of 10pt with clean sans-serif typography, structured grid alignments, and high-contrast color palettes suitable for dim judging rooms.
- **Mandatory Header Verification**: Ensure your 4-digit or 5-digit FTC Team Number and Team Name appear prominently in the header or footer of every single page.
