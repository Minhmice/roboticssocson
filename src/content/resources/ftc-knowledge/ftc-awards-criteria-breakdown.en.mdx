## Breaking Down Inspire and Think Award Criteria

![Robotics competition arena with teams and robots](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1485827404703-89b55fcc595e?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

Awards in FIRST Tech Challenge evaluate far more than match scores. Judges look for technical rigor, team culture, community impact, and documented proof that design decisions drove real iteration. Seasonal rubrics evolve, so treat this breakdown as an engineering preparation model rather than an official rule substitute. Always consult current FIRST manuals before competition.

### Inspire Award: Balanced Excellence Across Pillars

The Inspire Award recognizes top-tier all-around teams. Winning submissions balance three parallel tracks: a competitive robot, transparent team operations, and sustained STEM outreach outside competition pits. Judges look for evidence showing how every member contributes, how knowledge transfers across seasons, and how outreach metrics reflect genuine change rather than one-off photo opportunities.

| Assessment Area | Core Question Judges Ask | Concrete Evidence to Present |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engineering | How did data guide your robot architecture? | CAD models, design logs, cycle time tests, stress plots |
| Operations | How does the team manage resources and recruitment? | Skill matrices, team budget, onboarding schedule, retros |
| Outreach | What measurable difference was made outside FTC? | Event logs, attendee demographics, partner testimonials |
| Sustainability | Can this team repeat its success next season? | Multi-year funding plans, documentation handoffs, mentor logs |

### Think Award: Tracing Engineering Design Process

The Think Award honors teams who master iterative problem solving. Judges do not want a tidy after-the-fact narrative; they look for hypotheses, controlled bench tests, failure logs, and quantitative trade-off matrices. Showing why an intake or slide design failed—and how instrumented testing corrected the trajectory—proves technical maturity faster than unverified claims of perfection.

### Interview Strategy and Judge Interactions

- Assign clear sub-system owners so every student speaks authoritatively on their contribution.
- Maintain a single shared terminology across portfolio, pit presentation, and notebook.
- Answer questions with a direct conclusion, then immediately offer primary evidence or test charts.
- Avoid memorized scripts that fall apart when judges probe edge cases.
- Treat every pit interaction as an extension of the formal interview room.

### Pre-Event Checklist

- Cross-check current season rubrics and flag pages matching each item.
- Audit all outreach numbers for verifiable dates, contact leads, and methodology.
- Ensure CAD renders show internal mechanisms, sensor mounts, and wire routing.
- Keep battery health logs and mechanical inspection sheets ready on pit tables.
