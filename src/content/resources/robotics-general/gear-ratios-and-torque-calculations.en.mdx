## Gear Ratios and Mechanical Advantage

Gear reductions exchange rotational velocity for output torque, matching motor performance curves to robot mechanism loads. Increasing mechanical advantage allows lifting heavier manipulators or driving loaded chassis up ramps, but directly reduces linear velocity. Selecting gearboxes requires analyzing continuous operating points rather than isolated stall torque values.

![Meshing spur gears in motion](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Gears%20animation.gif)

### Core Transmission Equations

For driving gear tooth count `N_in` and driven gear tooth count `N_out`, gear ratio `G` is defined as `G = N_out / N_in`. In compound gearboxes with multiple sequential reduction stages, overall ratio is product of individual stage ratios: `G_total = G_1 × G_2 × ... × G_k`. Idler gears reverse rotation direction without altering the overall speed ratio between first input and final output.

| Parameter | Governing Formula | Metric Units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output Speed | `n_out = n_in / G` | RPM |
| Effective Output Torque | `T_out = T_in × G × η` | N·m |
| Linear Drive Force | `F_drive = T_out / r_wheel` | N |
| Linear Robot Speed | `v = (2π × r_wheel × n_out) / 60` | m/s |

Here `η` represents mechanical transmission efficiency (typically 0.85–0.90 per spur gear stage, lower for worm drives or planetary boxes with tight tolerances), and `r_wheel` represents wheel radius in meters. Overall efficiency compounds across stages: `η_total = η_1 × η_2 × ... × η_k`.

### Worked Drivetrain Sizing Example

Consider robot chassis driven by planetary motors delivering 0.40 N·m rated operating torque at 300 RPM. Applying external 3:1 spur reduction (`N_in = 12`, `N_out = 36`) yields total ratio `G = 3.0`. Assuming total gearbox efficiency `η = 0.85`:

- Output axle velocity: `n_out = 300 / 3 = 100 RPM`.
- Output axle torque: `T_out = 0.40 × 3 × 0.85 = 1.02 N·m`.
- With 0.05 m (50 mm) radius drive wheels: linear tractive force per motor reaches `F = 1.02 / 0.05 = 20.4 N`.
- Theoretical linear surface speed reaches `v = (2π × 0.05 × 100) / 60 ≈ 0.52 m/s`.

### Torque Margins and Load Verification

- Account for static friction, carpet rolling resistance, and incline loading. Tractive capability remains bounded by tire coefficient of friction `μ × Normal Force`.
- For rotating arms, calculate maximum gravitational load torque at horizontal extension: `T_gravity = m × g × d_com`, where `d_com` is horizontal distance between pivot axis and center of mass.
- Never operate brushed or brushless motors near stall torque under sustained loads; current spikes generate heat, trip circuit breakers, or destroy motor coils. Maintain continuous draw within safe power distribution limits.
- Check shaft shear stresses, keyway integrity, and tooth shear fatigue ratings. Maintain proper backlash spacing and tooth lubrication to avoid premature failure.
